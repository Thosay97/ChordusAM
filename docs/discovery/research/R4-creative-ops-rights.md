# R4: Creative operations, rights and channel revenue

**Date:** 9 November 2026 · **Author:** R4, Market Researcher · **Upstream read:** `docs/discovery/00-brief.md`, `docs/PLAN.md` (sections 1, 6, 9, 11, 12, 16)

**Summary.** This note looks at creative-operations features that most ERPs leave out and that a music-video studio needs. It covers six areas: client review and approval, a lean deliverables register (a light form of digital asset management, or DAM), rights and royalties under Sri Lankan law, cost tracking for AI-hybrid production, WhatsApp notifications, and social/SEO retainers. The main finding is that ChordusAM should **not** rebuild Frame.io or a full DAM. Those markets are mature and cheap at AMPL's size. ChordusAM's edge is to be the **system of record that links the money to the creative facts**: who approved which cut and when, which rights AMPL holds in each deliverable and until when, which talent consented to what (including AI likeness), and which AI credits went into which video. No tool I found for a small Sri Lankan studio joins these up. The highest-risk area is legal. Ownership of commissioned work, performers' royalties and AI likeness are all unsettled or under change in Sri Lanka in 2026, so the system should record what each contract says rather than assume a default.

> **Research limits.** The session's network blocked direct page fetches (vendor sites, NIPO, ICTA, Meta). Everything below comes from web-search results, and I have cited the pages they returned. Vendor prices often conflict between sources. Where they do, I give the range and mark it "(unverified)". Nothing here is legal advice. Every legal point needs a Sri Lankan IP lawyer to confirm it before it goes into a contract template or a rule in `lib/rules/`.

### 1.2 Patterns users already expect

1. **No reviewer account.** Clients open a link and comment (Vimeo, Frame.io). Asking a Sri Lankan client to sign up will fail.
2. **Comments are tied to a timecode or frame**, not "around 1:20".
3. **Version stacks.** v1, v2 and v3 sit under one item, and earlier comments are kept.
4. **A small set of statuses** (In review, Needs changes, Approved) plus **due dates and reminders** (Filestage).
5. **Audit evidence.** Who approved which version and when, kept as long as the contract needs it (Ziflow even sells retention by tier).

### 1.3 What it means for ChordusAM

- **Integrate, don't rebuild, the player.** Frame-accurate video review is hard to build well (transcoding, streaming, annotations). At 12 to 15 videos a month a Frame.io Pro or Vimeo seat costs little. Building it would take months.
- **Do own the approval record.** ChordusAM should keep a `deliverable_versions` row per cut (link to the Frame.io, Vimeo or Drive asset, version number, sent at) and an **approval event**: who approved, which version, when, through which channel, plus a note. Store it append-only, with the `audit_row_change()` trigger, as the CLAUDE.md rules require for sensitive tables.
- **Why this matters to AMPL:** approval is a **billing trigger** and a **dispute shield**. "Final cut approved" can release the balance invoice (PLAN.md 6.1) and close the producer's project stage. Change requests after approval can be flagged as billable extra rounds. AMPL's "48h turnaround" claim also needs approval timestamps to measure it.
- **Revision rounds as policy data.** The number of free revision rounds per package (for example 2) should be data edited in the UI, per the CLAUDE.md rule on company policy. It should not be hardcoded.
- **Optional later:** use Frame.io webhooks ([V4 webhooks](https://next.developer.frame.io/platform/docs/guides/webhooks)) to pull comment and status events automatically. The V4 API's limits on custom fields mean it should be a later phase, not the MVP.

### 2.2 A lean "deliverables register" (what ChordusAM should hold)

Leave the files in Drive, Dropbox or Frame.io and keep the **facts** in ChordusAM. One row per deliverable, with versions under it:

| Field group             | Fields                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity                | project, client, brand or channel (The Studio LK, Varnam TV and so on), type (music video, ad, photo set, song master, stems, thumbnail, reel), title, language (Sinhala, Tamil, English)                                       |
| Versions                | version number, storage link, checksum (optional), sent to client at, status, approval event (section 1.3)                                                                                                                      |
| Formats                 | output specs (16:9 4K, 9:16 1080p, WAV 48k, and so on), platform it is cut for                                                                                                                                                  |
| Ownership and rights    | who owns the video copyright, who owns the master, the composition owner(s), AMPL's licence scope (media, territory, term start and end), exclusivity, whether **Content ID claiming is allowed**, link to the signed agreement |
| Inputs and dependencies | talent releases used (section 3.4), licensed music or stock, AI tools used and the AI-likeness consents relied on (section 4)                                                                                                   |
| Expiry                  | usage-rights end date, reminder lead time (policy data, like Canto's 3 days but configurable), action on expiry (notify, take down, renew)                                                                                      |
| Publishing              | where published (YouTube video ID, Facebook, TikTok), publish date, AI-disclosure flag set (section 4.4)                                                                                                                        |

**Why it matters to AMPL:** at 12 to 15 music videos a month plus photo and ad work, AMPL will create several hundred deliverables a year. The two questions that cost money later are "do we still have the right to use this in that ad?" and "who signed off on this?". A register answers both without a DAM licence.

## 3. Rights, licensing and royalties (Sri Lanka focus)

### 3.1 The layers of rights in one music video

A single AMPL music video can hold **five separate layers of rights**. Each can belong to a different person.

| Layer                                              | Typical holder                                                                          | Notes                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Composition (lyrics and melody)                    | Lyricist, composer, or their publisher                                                  | Public performance is licensed in Sri Lanka through a collective society (section 3.3)             |
| Sound recording (the master)                       | Whoever paid for the recording, under contract (label, artist or AMPL)                  | Needed for any sync into video                                                                     |
| Performance (the singer or musician)               | The performer                                                                           | Sri Lanka recognises singers as performers, but there is no collecting body for them (section 3.3) |
| Audiovisual work (the video)                       | Under SL law, the producer is reported as first owner unless agreed otherwise (see 3.2) | This is what Content ID claims on YouTube                                                          |
| Likeness and personality (face, voice, AI replica) | The person                                                                              | Handled by contract and consent, and by personal-data law from 2027                                |

### 3.2 Ownership under the Intellectual Property Act No. 36 of 2003

- **Default rule:** the author is the original owner of economic rights (s. 14(1)) ([Legal 500, Sri Lanka IP guide](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/sri-lanka-intellectual-property/?export-pdf), [Wikipedia, Copyright law of Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_law_of_Sri_Lanka)).
- **Audiovisual works:** secondary sources say that for audiovisual works the **producer is first owner unless otherwise agreed** (Legal 500, Wikipedia citing s. 14). I could not read the statute text (unverified).
- **Employees and commissioned works:** s. 14(3) and (4) were repealed and replaced by an amendment around 2013. The new (4) deals with works by employees in the course of employment. A fragment refers to "the person who commissioned the work" ([NIPO amendment PDF](https://www.nipo.gov.lk/web/images/ddg/news_/ip_act_amendmend.pdf), [Supreme Court judgment](https://supremecourt.lk/wp-content/uploads/judgements/sc_chc_appeal_05_2013.pdf)). The exact scope is **unverified**.
- **Term:** life of the author plus 70 years. Sources disagree on the term for audiovisual works and sound recordings (from publication or from making) ([Wikimedia Commons, SL rules](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Sri_Lanka)).
- **Industry practice elsewhere:** the commissioning label usually owns the video, and the director waives or assigns rights. Work-for-hire status is **not automatic** without a written agreement made before the shoot. In _Sisyphus Touring v TMZ_ a US court found no work for hire because there was no prior written agreement ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/zh-hans/insights/publications/2016/09/sisyphus-touring-inc-v-tmz-productions-inc)). Australian guidance says the agreement should state who owns the production assets ([Arts Law Centre factsheet](https://artslaw.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/11_Video_Clip_Filming_Agreement_Factsheet.pdf)).

**Implication:** AMPL cannot rely on a default. Some videos are commissioned by artists or labels, and some are made for AMPL's own channels. The answer to "who owns this video and master?" must be a **recorded field per deliverable, taken from the signed agreement**. ChordusAM should block "publish to own channel" or "enable Content ID" when that field is blank. Contract templates need a lawyer.

| **Licensing monopoly**                                           | Only a collective society registered with NIPO may carry on the business of granting such licences (per the same search results, unverified against the Act)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Performers (singers)**                                         | "There is currently no central mechanism in Sri Lanka to collect royalties for singers and performers." **Implication for AMPL:** local collective royalties are small. **YouTube and streaming are where Sinhala and Tamil music earns** (Interspace Music: "the audience is online, and it is mostly YouTube"). ChordusAM should track **royalty and revenue splits per song and per video** (AMPL, artist, lyricist, composer) and the **statements received** from YouTube, the distributor and ACPOSL. It does not need to become a royalty-accounting engine. A performers' CMO may appear in 2027, so keep the "rights party" model general.

### 3.4 Talent and model releases

I found no Sri Lankan rule specific to model releases. The **Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022** takes effect for key provisions on **1 January 2027** (Extraordinary Gazette 2498/16 of 22 July 2026). The amending law is Act No. 22 of 2025 ([Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Data-protection-compliance-regime-takes-effect-on-1-Jan-2027/44-795776), [Neelakandan](https://neelakandan.lk/insights/data-protection-in-transition-understanding-sri-lankas-new-legal-framework)). The Act is consent-based and gives a right to withdraw consent ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=LK&t=law)). A practical release should state the **purpose, media, territory, term and how to withdraw**. ChordusAM should hold each release as a record with those fields, linked to the talent's vendor or person record and to the deliverables that use it. This links to SEC's file `docs/discovery/15-compliance-security.md`.

### 4.3 Per-project AI cost attribution (proposed approach)

PLAN.md already records AI subscriptions as USD expenses with an FX rate (section 6.2) and wants per-video profitability (section 6.3). What is missing is **allocation of shared subscriptions to projects**. Three levels, from simple to precise:

1. **Pool and allocate (MVP, S):** each month, split each subscription's LKR cost across that month's projects using a driver: equal split, a weight by video length, or the producer's estimate. The allocation rule is policy data.
2. **Usage log (M):** producers log credits, downloads and GPU hours per project in a quick mobile form, and ChordusAM values them at the plan's effective cost per credit (subscription ÷ credits). This also shows **wasted credits** (bought but unused).
3. **API import (L, later):** pull usage where a vendor offers an API, for example Runway's API. Most consumer plans do not expose per-generation usage.

**Why it matters to AMPL:** AI tools are a USD cost on a LKR price list. Without attribution, AMPL cannot tell whether a LKR-priced music-video package still makes money after FX moves or a vendor price rise.

### 4.4 Consent, disclosure and AI-likeness rules

**ChordusAM need:** an **AI-likeness consent record** per person (scope: face, voice, or both; projects or "any AMPL project"; term; pay; withdrawal date). A deliverable cannot be marked "published" while it uses a likeness without a valid consent. The disclosure flag is required on publish.

## 5. Client and talent communication (WhatsApp, portals)

### 5.1 WhatsApp Business Platform

- **Pricing model:** per message since 1 July 2025, with volume tiers for utility and authentication templates ([Meta developer docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)).
- **From 1 October 2026:** free-form **service messages and utility templates sent inside the 24-hour customer-service window are now charged** at the market's utility rate, with no volume discount. Customer-sent messages stay free ([Twilio notice](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/53100480177819-Notice-Changes-to-WhatsApp-s-Pricing-October-2026), [respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-pricing-change-2026)).
- **Sri Lanka:** moved to its own rates on 1 October 2026, with **lower utility and authentication rates** and a new, higher "authentication-international" rate ([Meta developer docs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). Third-party figures: marketing about **$0.0732**, utility and authentication about **$0.0020**, authentication-international about $0.1440 per message ([RichAutomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/whatsapp-api-pricing-by-country-2026), [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/09/15/meta-charges-pakistan-11x-more-than-india-for-whatsapp-business-messages/)) (unverified).
- **Templates:** business-initiated messages outside the window must use a pre-approved template in the marketing, utility or authentication category. Sending a template does not open the window ([Hello Charles](https://help.hello-charles.com/en/articles/210024-understanding-the-whatsapp-24-hour-service-window-and-templates)). Business Solution Providers (BSPs) add their own fees on top of Meta's.
- **Cost illustration:** 3,000 utility messages a month (leave decisions, payment-paid notices, "cut v2 ready for review", booking reminders) × $0.002 ≈ **$6 a month** plus BSP fees (unverified). Message fees are not the barrier. **Template approval and opt-in** are the work.

**Fit with PLAN.md section 11:** PLAN.md lists email and in-app notifications. WhatsApp should be an **optional third channel** for the same events, using **utility templates only** (no marketing), with an opt-in stored per person, as PDPA consent requires.

### 5.2 Client portal patterns

- Review without an account (Vimeo, Frame.io) is the bar.
- Per-client white-label portals for reports ([AgencyAnalytics](https://agencyanalytics.com/pricing)).
- **For ChordusAM:** a light client view using magic-link sign-in or a signed link. It would show project status, versions to review (linking out to the player), an "approve" button that writes the approval event, invoices and receipts, and retainer usage. Access must follow the CLAUDE.md rules: server guards plus RLS scoped to `client_id`. Hiding the page is not protection.
