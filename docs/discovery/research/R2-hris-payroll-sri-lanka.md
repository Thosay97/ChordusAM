# R2: HRIS, payroll and attendance for a small Sri Lankan media studio

**Author:** R2, People Ops SME (discovery team) | **Date:** 9 November 2026 | **Upstream read:** `docs/discovery/00-brief.md`, `docs/PLAN.md` sections 1, 2, 4, 7, 9, 10, 12, 15, 16.

**Summary.** AMPL is a 10 to 30 person Colombo studio with a large freelancer pool. Global HRIS products (BambooHR, HiBob, Rippling, Sage HR, Keka, greytHR, Zoho) are either too expensive for this size or lack confirmed Sri Lankan statutory payroll. Sri Lankan SaaS products (Humanised, Gallery HR, LankaPayslip, MiHCM Lite, PeoplesHR and others) do cover EPF, ETF and APIT, at roughly LKR 10,000 to 25,000 a month for this headcount where prices are public. None of them handles AMPL's real differentiator: freelancers paid per project, linked to productions, studio bookings and rights. The statutory picture as of November 2026 has five points that matter for ChordusAM. (1) APIT is a monthly employer deduction from a published IRD table, paid by the 15th of the next month, with an annual statement that now needs each employee's TIN. (2) The EPF base is "total earnings", not basic salary, so each pay component needs an "EPF-liable" flag. (3) Gratuity becomes a legal liability once the company has 15 or more workers on any day in a 12-month period, which AMPL is close to. (4) The core of the Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022 takes effect on **1 January 2027**. (5) A 5% withholding tax applies to some service fees paid to resident individuals above LKR 100,000 a month, and its scope was widened in June 2026. That matters for freelancer payouts. PLAN.md covers attendance, leave and a simple payroll well. It does not yet cover APIT, the EPF earnings base, gratuity, statutory leave floors, overtime pay, payslip content, statutory exports, freelancer contracts and talent records, onboarding and offboarding, documents, appraisals, or PDPA retention.

> **Research limits in this session.** Direct page fetches were blocked for government hosts (ird.gov.lk, epf.lk, assets.kpmg.com did not resolve), and the team's shared web-search budget ran out during Part C. Every citation below comes from search-result text for the linked page. Items marked **(unverified)** could not be confirmed. Items marked **(secondary)** come from a payroll vendor, blog or aggregator, not from a government or law-firm source. Before any rule is coded, the accountant or lawyer should check it against the primary text. Under the project rules (CLAUDE.md), rates and thresholds must be data edited in the UI, so a correction does not need a code change.

**Takeaways.**

1. None of the big global HRIS products has confirmed native Sri Lankan payroll. Deel covers SL only by becoming the legal employer (EOR), at about $599 per head per month. That is far too expensive for AMPL's own staff, though it could be an option for one-off foreign hires.
2. **Odoo** and **Frappe HR** are the only global options with a credible SL path. Odoo has a third-party SL payroll module, and Frappe is open source with ZKTeco sync. Both mean running a second ERP next to ChordusAM, which works against the "one system" goal in PLAN.md section 1.
3. Deel's and Rippling's contractor products show what a freelancer module should do: contracts, invoices, compliance checks and multi-currency payouts. That is a good reference for the ChordusAM vendor and talent design.

**What this means for AMPL.**

- At AMPL's size, local SaaS payroll costs roughly **LKR 10,000 to 25,000 a month** (Humanised's public price) or about **LKR 4,000 to 15,000 a month** at LKR 400 to 500 per employee for 10 to 30 staff (secondary). That is the price ceiling a built payroll module in ChordusAM has to beat on total cost of ownership. The CFO (`docs/discovery/14-business-case.md`) should use these figures.
- Local vendors win on statutory upkeep: they update the APIT tables when the IRD changes them. If ChordusAM builds payroll, someone has to own that update every April, and whenever the law is amended (for example Act No. 11 of 2026).
- No local vendor found offers a **freelancer and talent pool linked to projects**. That gap is where ChordusAM's value lies.

**How an employer applies it.** Each month, take the APIT-liable earnings, look up the tax in the right table (Table 01 for a regular monthly salary), and deduct that amount. Bonuses and lump sums use the cumulative or lump-sum tables. The tables include grossing-up variants for employers that pay the tax on the employee's behalf. That matters because PLAN.md allows "net" contracts (for example LKR 85,000 net). A net contract means AMPL bears the tax and has to gross it up. **The specific table numbers for gross-up were not verified this session.**

**For ChordusAM:** hold APIT tables as **versioned data** (effective from and to dates, bands, relief), never in code. `computeApit()` should be a pure function in `lib/rules/`, with unit tests that reproduce the IRD's published worked examples. Add a **TIN** field to employees.

### B.3 Withholding tax on freelancer service fees (important for AMPL)

- From **3 June 2026**, the **5% WHT on service fees paid to resident individuals** was extended to more types of service provider, including writer and translator. It applies when the monthly payment to that person **exceeds LKR 100,000** ([Ada Derana](https://adaderana.lk/news/cmqqg69mt0002356p19t9drsg), [FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/IRD-outlines-sweeping-tax-changes/44-793700), [regfollower](https://regfollower.com/sri-lanka-ird-issues-guidance-on-withholding-tax-deduction/)).
- IRD circular SEC/2026/E/04 (8 June 2026) lists **14% on service fees paid to non-residents** (secondary summary, [regfollower](https://regfollower.com/sri-lanka-ird-issues-guidance-on-withholding-tax-deduction/)). This could apply to overseas talent or GPU and AI vendors. It needs checking against the AI-tool subscriptions (R3).
- **(unverified)**: the full list of service types in s.85(1C), and whether vocalists, musicians, editors, videographers and "AI artists" are on it. This is a direct input to PLAN.md open question 10 ("does your accountant need withholding tax recorded?"). The answer is probably yes, for at least some freelancers.

### B.5 Maternity leave

### B.10 Payslips, record keeping and contractor classification

- **Payslips:** one secondary source says employers must keep wage records and give itemised payslips ([remotepeople](https://remotepeople.com/countries/sri-lanka/employer-of-record/minimum-wage/)). The Wages Boards Ordinance has a "wage records" section ([srilankalaw.lk](https://www.srilankalaw.lk/revised-statutes/alphabetical-list-of-statutes/1683-wages-boards-ordinance-2.html)). **No statutory SL payslip format or field list was confirmed (unverified).** Good practice is to show gross components, EPF 8% deducted, APIT deducted, other deductions with reasons, net pay, and employer EPF 12% and ETF 3% for information, plus the employee's EPF number and the pay period.
- **Record retention:** **not confirmed** for the labour laws or the Inland Revenue Act **(unverified)**. Make retention a configurable setting per record type, with a default agreed with the accountant. This also feeds the PDPA retention duty (B.11).
- **Contractor vs employee:** Sri Lankan courts use **several tests together**: control, integration, economic reality or dependence, and who owns the equipment. The label in the contract does not decide it ([Conventus case study](https://conventuslaw.com/report/sri-lanka-analyzing-the-employment-consultant-divide-a-case-study-on-unfair-termination-in-sri-lankan-law), [SLLR v3 248](https://lankalaw.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/v3-sri-lr-248.pdf)). If a "contractor" is found to be an employee, EPF and ETF arrears plus surcharges (up to 50%) follow. A claim that there is no time limit on the lookback is **(unverified)**.
  - **Risk for AMPL:** PLAN.md's "Part-time Contractor" template has fixed office days, fingerprint attendance and absence deductions. That is strong evidence of control and integration, which points toward employee status. PLAN.md section 10 already warns about this. The system should go further: record a **classification checklist** per engagement, signed off by Admin, with the date and the accountant's or lawyer's name, and keep it in the audit log. Freelancers paid per deliverable (vocalists, session musicians, editors on piece-rate) should live in the **vendor and talent** side, not on payroll.

### B.11 Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022 (as amended by Act No. 22 of 2025)

- **Commencement:** Extraordinary Gazette **No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026** brings **sections 2 and 3 and Parts I and III into force on 1 January 2027**. Part I is the processing rules: lawful basis, purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy, retention limits, integrity and confidentiality, and accountability. Part III covers controller and processor duties: Data Protection Officer, breach notification and impact assessments. **Part II (data subject rights), Part IV (direct marketing) and Part VII (penalties) wait for a later order** ([FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Data-protection-compliance-regime-takes-effect-on-1-Jan-2027/44-795776), [Gazette 2498/16](https://documents.gov.lk/view/egz/2026/7/2498-16_E.pdf), [Sunday Times](https://www.sundaytimes.lk/260809/business-times/action-for-compliance-as-dpa-enforces-next-year-651378.html), [Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/breaking-news/Legal-experts-flag-unique-compliance-landscape-in-staggered-PDPA-rollout/108-346685)). Some older guides give 18 March 2025, which the July 2026 gazette replaces.
- The Data Protection Authority has existed since 2023 and can already investigate and issue directives ([DPA](https://www.dpa.gov.lk/)).
- **HR implications for ChordusAM (my reading, for SEC to confirm in `docs/discovery/15-compliance-security.md`):**
  1. A **legal basis per data category**: employment contract for payroll, legal obligation for EPF, ETF and APIT, and legitimate interest or consent for attendance and photos.
  2. **Biometrics:** keep fingerprint and face templates **on the device only**. ChordusAM should receive only device user ID, timestamp and verify mode. Biometric data is likely a special category under the PDPA **(unverified this session)**.
  3. A **retention schedule** with automatic flagging for deletion. Ex-employees' and rejected candidates' data must not be kept forever.
  4. **Name a DPO** (it can be an internal person) and write a **breach-response procedure**. The breach-notification duty is in Part III, which starts in January 2027.
  5. A **privacy notice** to staff and freelancers at onboarding, with its acknowledgement stored.
  6. **Cross-border transfer:** Supabase and Vercel host data outside Sri Lanka. Check the Act's cross-border rules and the DPA's adequacy and contract-clause guidance **(unverified)**. SEC and SA should review.
  7. PLAN.md section 12 (encrypt NIC and bank details, audit views) is a good base. Add **access logging for reads** of salary, NIC and bank data, not just writes.

### D.4 What PLAN.md misses (consolidated)

1. **No APIT** (B.2): net-pay contracts cannot be correct without a tax engine and gross-up.
2. **EPF base is undefined** (B.1): needs component-level liability flags.
3. **No statutory identifiers**: EPF no., TIN (now mandatory in the APIT annual statement), employer registrations.
4. **No statutory outputs**: Form C / e-C, ETF Form II, APIT schedules, T10.
5. **No gratuity** (B.6): AMPL is likely at the 15-workmen threshold.
6. **No overtime or holiday-work pay** (B.4).
7. **No statutory floors or minimum-wage validation** (B.4, B.5, B.7).
8. **No final settlement / offboarding** flow.
9. **No recruitment or onboarding**, and no **employee documents** beyond payslips.
10. **No appraisals / probation review** workflow, even though probation end dates are tracked.
11. **Freelancers are only vendors**: no talent profile, contracts, rights or consent forms, classification checks, or WHT (D.3).
12. **PDPA operative 1 January 2027**: section 12 needs a retention schedule, read logging, a DPO, notices and a breach log.
13. **Single entity**: no `entity_id` for a future SG entity.
14. **Attendance**: no `device_serial` and no path to ADMS push or mobile check-in for location shoots.
15. **Absence deduction rule** (LKR 4,000 a day) needs legal confirmation under the Shop and Office Act's deduction limits.

### D.5 Build vs buy, from the People Ops angle (input for CFO and SA)

- **Buy-only** (for example Humanised, about LKR 10,000 to 25,000 a month): fastest statutory compliance, with the vendor maintaining the tables. But it is a second system for HR, and it leaves freelancers, projects and rights unsolved.
- **Build-only in ChordusAM:** one system and full fit for freelancers and projects. But AMPL then owns the statutory upkeep (APIT tables, rate changes, gazette holidays) and the legal risk of a calculation error.
- **Hybrid (my recommendation for the MVP):** build core HR, attendance, leave, the freelancer and talent pool, and payroll _preparation_ in ChordusAM, with statutory rates and tables as data and an accountant sign-off step. Produce EPF, ETF and APIT figures and exports. Keep the option to hand the run to a local payroll bureau or SaaS through an export if the accountant prefers. Review after one full tax year.
